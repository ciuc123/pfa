# Phase 4: Delivery — Building Your Repeatable Process

**Duration:** 5–10 days
**Goal:** Deliver your first customer successfully and document the repeatable process
**Output:** Completed first delivery + process documentation + testimonial/case study

---

## Why This Phase Matters

Validation and design are planning. Delivery is reality. This phase is where you actually solve the problem for a real customer.

This phase is not about perfection. It's about:
1. **Delivering exactly what you promised** (no scope creep)
2. **Documenting every step** (so you can repeat it)
3. **Getting proof** (testimonial, case study, metrics)
4. **Improving the process** (what took too long? what worked well?)

---

## Exercise 1: Land Your First Customer

**Time: 2–7 days**

You already have prospects from Phase 2 who said "yes, I'd pay for this." Now close one.

**The close process:**

1. **Message them back:** "You mentioned this was valuable. I'm ready to help. Here's what I'd do for you [product spec]. Price: €[X]. Interested in a quick call to discuss?"

2. **Schedule a call:** Use Calendly or just propose 2–3 times.

3. **Run the "close call":** (15–30 minutes)
   - Confirm the problem hasn't changed
   - Walk them through your spec
   - Ask: "Does this solve your problem?"
   - Ask: "Are you ready to move forward?"
   - If yes: "Great. Let's get started. I'll send you an invoice and we'll kick off on [date]."

4. **Send invoice + onboarding details**

5. **Confirm start date and next steps**

**Close call script:**

```
"Hey [Name], thanks for taking the time. Based on our earlier conversation, I understand the main issue is [their problem]. 

Here's what I propose: [your spec in plain language].

This would take [timeline] and cost €[price].

Does that feel like it would actually solve what you're dealing with?"

[Listen to their response]

"Great—I'm excited to help. Let's get this started. I'll send you an invoice today, and we can kick off on [date]. Sound good?"
```

**Invoice note:**

Keep it simple. One line: "[Product name]: €[price]" 

Don't overcomplicate. This is a service delivery, not a complex contract.

**Template:**

```
Invoice to: [Company name]
For: [Your product/service name]
Amount: €[price]
Payment due: [date, usually 7 days]
Project starts: [date]

Payment to: [Your bank details or payment link]
```

## Exercise 2: Execute Your Delivery Process

**Time:** Depends on your scope (typically 1–4 weeks)

Follow the process you documented in Phase 3. Do it exactly as planned.

**The execution checklist:**

```
[ ] Week 1 - Kickoff
  [ ] Send kickoff meeting link
  [ ] Send questionnaire/info request
  [ ] Conduct kickoff call (take notes)
  [ ] Confirm you have everything you need to proceed
  [ ] Send "what happens next" email

[ ] Week 2 - Main Work (or Weeks 2-3 depending on your scope)
  [ ] Complete the core work (audit, analysis, creation, etc.)
  [ ] Document your findings
  [ ] Identify the solutions/recommendations
  [ ] [Your specific tasks here]

[ ] Week 3/4 - Deliverables
  [ ] Create the report/deliverable
  [ ] Quality check (is this good? would you be proud to share this?)
  [ ] Send draft to customer
  [ ] Collect feedback
  [ ] Finalize

[ ] Week 4/5 - Follow-up
  [ ] Schedule follow-up call
  [ ] Present findings/deliverables
  [ ] Answer questions
  [ ] Document what they're going to do next
  [ ] Send final deliverables + any extras
  [ ] Thank them, ask for testimonial

[ ] Post-delivery
  [ ] Wait 2 weeks for them to implement
  [ ] Follow-up email: "How's it going? Any results yet?"
  [ ] Ask for testimonial + metrics
```

**Key rule: Deliver on time.** If you said 3 weeks, deliver in 3 weeks. Be reliable.

## Exercise 3: Capture Proof from Your First Customer

**Time: 1–2 hours**

Don't move forward until you have proof. You need:

1. **Testimonial:** A quote from them about your work (3–5 sentences)
2. **Metrics:** What improved? (time saved, cost reduced, performance gained, risk reduced)
3. **Case study outline:** How you solved their specific problem

**How to ask for testimonial:**

Email template:

```
Subject: Quick favor — could use your feedback

Hi [Name],

I loved working with you on [product name]. Would you mind spending 2 minutes writing a short testimonial about your experience? 

Just answer these 3 things (one sentence each):
1. What was the problem you were facing?
2. How did [my product] help?
3. What would you tell someone considering working with me?

Thanks—happy to return the favor if you ever need anything.

— [Your name]
```

**Metrics to ask for:**

```
"I'd love to know: did this move the needle for you? Any metrics?

For example:
- Time saved per week?
- Cost reduction?
- Performance improvement?
- Risk reduced?
- Team productivity gain?

Doesn't have to be precise—even rough estimates help."
```

**Template to fill in:**

```
**Testimonial:**
[Their quote]

**Metrics (before/after):**
- [Metric 1]: ___ → ___
- [Metric 2]: ___ → ___
- [Metric 3]: ___ → ___

**Case study headline:**
[The customer's specific situation + your solution + the result]

Example: "How [Company] Reduced API Response Times by 60% with an Audit & Optimization Sprint"
```

1. **What went well:**

## Exercise 5: Complete Deliveries 2 & 3 (Fast Track)

**Time: 10–20 days** (if you do back-to-back deliveries)

Now do it again. Twice.

**Why 3 deliveries before Phase 5:**

- ✅ Customer 1: You nail the process (slow, careful)
- ✅ Customer 2: You start to speed up (familiar now)
- ✅ Customer 3: You have proof this is repeatable

After 3 completions, you should be 40–60% faster than Customer 1.

**Fast-track strategy:**

- Start Customer 2 before Customer 1 is completely done (if scope allows)
- Use the same templates/process for all three
- Time-box each delivery (don't let scope creep)
- Improve the process between each one (but don't redesign)

**For customers 2 & 3:**

- Same process as Customer 1
- Higher price (€750 for #2, €1,000–1,200 for #3)
- Same testimonial request
- Same retrospective

**Tracking template for 3 deliveries:**


## Exercise 6: Build Your Case Study

**Time: 1–2 hours**

After all 3 deliveries, you have enough data for a strong case study.

**Case study template:**

```markdown
# [Customer Name]: How We [Achieved Specific Result]

## The Situation
[What was the customer's problem? Be specific.]

2–3 paragraphs describing:
- Their challenge
- Why it mattered (impact)
- What they'd tried before

## What We Did
[Your approach/process in plain language]

- Step 1: ___
- Step 2: ___
- Step 3: ___

(Keep it short. What actually changed for them?)

## The Results

[Metrics: what changed?]

- Metric 1: X → Y
- Metric 2: X → Y
- Metric 3: X → Y

## What They Say

"[Testimonial from customer]"

— [Name], [Title], [Company]

## Key Takeaway

[One sentence on the lesson learned or approach that worked]
```

**Real Example:**

```markdown
# AgencyXYZ: Reducing API Response Times by 60% in 10 Days

## The Situation

AgencyXYZ manages Laravel backends for 5+ SaaS clients. Their biggest challenge: performance issues that spike randomly, costing them client complaints and internal debugging hours.

One of their key clients was experiencing 2–3 second response times during peak load, resulting in a 40% bounce rate on critical pages. The team suspected database issues and caching problems, but didn't have the expertise to debug systematically.

## What We Did

We ran a focused 3-day audit:

- **Day 1:** Deep code review and database query analysis
- **Day 2:** Load testing and infrastructure review
- **Day 3:** Detailed findings and recommendations

We identified 7 specific bottlenecks: N+1 queries, missing database indexes, and suboptimal Redis caching.

## The Results

- Response time: 2.1 seconds → 800ms (62% improvement)
- Database queries per request: 45 → 8 (82% reduction)
- API stability: 88% uptime → 99.7% uptime
- Implementation time: 2 days (all quick wins)

## What They Say

"In 3 days, Alex identified issues we'd been chasing for 6 months. The recommendations were actionable and specific. We were able to implement them immediately without major risk."

— Marcus T., CTO, AgencyXYZ

## Key Takeaway

Systematic audits beat guesswork. One day of expert analysis saves weeks of internal debugging.
```

## Reflection Questions

1. **Confidence:** After 3 deliveries, on a scale of 1–10, how confident are you that you can keep delivering this consistently?
2. **Speed:** How much faster was delivery #3 than delivery #1? (Target: 40–60% faster)
3. **Repetability:** Could someone else follow your documented process and get 80% of your results?
4. **Profitability:** After all costs (tools, time, etc.), what's your margin per customer?
5. **Motivation:** Do you still want to do this 100 times? Or should you pivot?

---

## Common Mistakes in Phase 4

❌ **Scope creep** — Customer asks for extras, you deliver them for free. Set boundaries.

❌ **Perfectionism** — Spending 100 hours when 50 would suffice. Ship it.

❌ **Not documenting** — "I'll remember." You won't. Write it down as you go.

❌ **Skipping the testimonial** — Do NOT move forward without proof. Get it from all 3 customers.

❌ **Not improving between deliveries** — You should be 20%+ faster by delivery #3.

---

## How to Know You're Ready for Phase 5

✅ You've completed 3 full deliveries
✅ All 3 customers are satisfied (payment received, testimonials given)
✅ You have metrics/results from all 3
✅ You've documented your process (it's repeatable)
✅ You have 3+ testimonials and 1–2 case studies

If you have all 5 checkmarks, you're ready for [Phase 5: Launch](./05-launch.md).
