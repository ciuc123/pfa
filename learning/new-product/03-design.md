# Phase 3: Design — Building Your Product Spec & Pricing

**Duration:** 3–7 days
**Goal:** Define exactly what you're selling, how you'll deliver it, and what it costs
**Output:** Service specification + pricing model + delivery timeline

---

## Why This Phase Matters

Before you start building, you need to know:
- **What** you're delivering (the scope)
- **How** you're delivering it (your process)
- **How long** it takes (timeline)
- **How much** it costs (pricing)

Most founders get this wrong because they haven't actually talked to customers yet. You have. Use that data.

---

## Exercise 1: Design Your Product from Customer Input

**Time: 2 hours**

From Phase 2, you know what problems your customers have. Now design a solution that fixes them.

**The design template:**

```
**What It Solves:**
[The specific problem from Phase 2]

**Who It's For:**
[Your ICP: company type + role]

**What's Included:**
[The core deliverables - be specific]

**Timeline:**
[How many weeks/days? Week 1 do X, Week 2 do Y, etc]

**How It Works (Step by Step):**
1. ___________________
2. ___________________
3. ___________________
4. ___________________

**What Success Looks Like:**
[The outcome your customer gets: "API response time reduced by 60%", "Zero downtime deployments", etc]

**Price:**
[Initial price - see Exercise 3]

**Deliverables/Output:**
[What does the customer actually GET? A document? Training? Code? Access to something?]
```

**Real Example (API Performance Audit):**

```
**What It Solves:**
Laravel APIs timing out under load, causing downtime and lost revenue.

**Who It's For:**
Mid-size agencies (8–20 devs) with Laravel backends serving 500+ concurrent users.

**What's Included:**
- 2-hour live audit of their codebase, database, caching, infrastructure
- Detailed report with 5–10 specific recommendations
- Priority scoring (quick wins vs. long-term fixes)
- 1-hour follow-up call to explain recommendations

**Timeline:**
- Day 1: Initial code/data access + 2-hour audit
- Day 2–3: Write detailed report
- Day 4: Follow-up call + Q&A

Total: 4 days, 5–7 hours of consulting time

**How It Works:**
1. Kickoff call: understand current architecture, pain points, constraints
2. Deep dive: review code, database queries, caching logic, deployment
3. Analysis: identify bottlenecks (N+1 queries, missing indexes, bad caching)
4. Recommendations: write report with specific fixes and expected impact
5. Follow-up: present findings, answer questions, discuss implementation timeline

**What Success Looks Like:**
Customer has clear roadmap to reduce response times by 50%+. Can implement fixes immediately.

**Price:**
€1,200 (for first 3 customers)

**Deliverables:**
- Detailed audit report (20–30 pages)
- Recorded follow-up call
- Shared recommendations spreadsheet (for their team to track progress)
```

**Exercise: Write your design spec.**

Use the template above. Be as specific as possible about what you're delivering.

## Exercise 2: Stress-Test Your Scope

**Time: 1 hour**

Before you lock in your scope, ask yourself:

1. **Can you do this in 60 days?** (Total delivery time + support for first 3 customers)
2. **Can you do this 3 times in a row?** (Without going insane)
3. **Can you do this while employed?** (If you are employed — realistically?)
4. **Could a junior person follow your process?** (Is it documented enough? Repeatable?)

**Scope check:**

```
Deliverables I'm including:
1. ___________________
2. ___________________
3. ___________________
4. ___________________

Estimated time (be honest):
- Phase 1 (setup/kickoff): ___ hours
- Phase 2 (main work): ___ hours
- Phase 3 (deliverables/report): ___ hours
- Phase 4 (follow-up/support): ___ hours
- Total: ___ hours

Can I do this 3 times in 60 days? [Yes/No]
Can I document the process? [Yes/No]
Would I want to do this 100 times? [Yes/No/Maybe]
```

**If you answered "No" to any of these:**

- Your scope is too big. Remove 2–3 deliverables.
- Your timeline is too ambitious. Extend it.
- You're trying to be too perfect. Cut the nice-to-haves.

**Example of scope reduction:**

❌ Too big: "I'll do a full audit, implementation training, 3 months of support, and deploy their fixes."

✅ Right size: "I'll do the audit and 1 follow-up call. They implement based on my recommendations."

## Exercise 3: Set Your Pricing

**Time: 1 hour**

Pricing has three components: cost to deliver, market rate, and value to customer.

**The pricing formula:**

```
Cost to deliver: X hours × (your hourly rate / 2) = cost
Market rate: What competitors charge for similar work
Value to customer: How much does this problem cost them?

Pricing = Cost × 1.5 to Cost × 3, capped at market rate, never below value
```

**Example:**

```
Cost to deliver:
- My audit takes 7 hours
- My rate is €100/hour
- Cost = 7 × (100/2) = €350

Market rate:
- Similar audits from agencies: €1,500–3,000
- My rate: mid-range (€1,200–1,500)

Value to customer:
- One hour of downtime costs them €500–1,000
- One failed deployment costs them €2,000–5,000
- My audit prevents this: worth €5,000+ to them

My first price: €1,200 (cost + 3x markup, below market, way below customer value)
```

**Pricing for your first 3 customers:**

Use the "founder's price" (lower than final price to get social proof):

| Customer | Price | Why |
|----------|-------|-----|
| Customer 1 | €500–750 | "Founder price—help me build the case study" |
| Customer 2 | €750–1,000 | "Slightly higher, but still proof price" |
| Customer 3 | €1,000–1,500 | "Getting closer to market rate" |
| Customer 4+ | €1,500–3,000 | "Full price with case studies/testimonials" |

**Your pricing:**

```
Cost to deliver: € _____ (X hours × hourly rate / 2)
Market rate: € _____ to € _____ (check competitors)
Value to customer: € _____ (how much does the problem cost them?)

Founder price (first 3 customers): € _____
Regular price (customers 4+): € _____
```

**Pricing rules:**

- ✅ Charge something (even €500). Free customers don't give testimonials.
- ✅ Raise price after each customer (€500 → €750 → €1,000 → €1,500)
- ✅ Use the founder price to get case studies and social proof
- ❌ Don't charge market rate for your first customer (nobody knows you yet)
- ❌ Don't undercharge to the point that you resent the work

## Exercise 4: Create Your Delivery Process

**Time: 2–3 hours**

Write down exactly how you'll deliver this product. Every. Single. Step.

This is your repeatable system. After customer 1, you'll refine it. But start with a detailed outline.

**The process template:**

```
**Week 1: Kickoff**
- [ ] Send kickoff meeting link + questionnaire
- [ ] 1-hour kickoff call (understand their situation, pain points, constraints)
- [ ] Collect access credentials / information needed for audit
- [ ] Send "what to expect" email

**Week 2: Deep Dive**
- [ ] Audit/analysis work (your main work block)
- [ ] Document findings
- [ ] Identify solutions

**Week 3: Deliverables**
- [ ] Write report/create deliverable
- [ ] Internal review (is this good enough?)
- [ ] Send draft to customer
- [ ] Incorporate feedback

**Week 4: Follow-up**
- [ ] 1-hour call: present findings, answer questions
- [ ] Send final deliverables
- [ ] Offer limited follow-up support (1–2 emails)

**Tools you'll need:**
- ___________________
- ___________________
- ___________________

**Templates you'll create:**
- Kickoff questionnaire
- Audit checklist
- Report template
- Follow-up email

**Time breakdown:**
- Kickoff call: ___ hours
- Analysis: ___ hours
- Deliverable creation: ___ hours
- Follow-up call: ___ hours
- Total: ___ hours
```

**Real Example (API Audit):**

```
Week 1: Kickoff
- [ ] Send Calendly link for kickoff call
- [ ] Send "API Audit Questionnaire" (5 questions about architecture, pain points, scale)
- [ ] 1-hour kickoff: discuss current setup, get repo access, understand constraints
- [ ] Send "Here's what happens next" email with timeline

Week 2: Analysis
- [ ] Clone repo and review codebase (1.5 hours)
- [ ] Query database performance (0.5 hours)
- [ ] Test response times under load (1 hour)
- [ ] Review infrastructure/deployment (0.5 hours)
- [ ] Create audit spreadsheet with findings (1.5 hours)

Week 3: Report
- [ ] Write detailed report in Notion (3 hours)
- [ ] Create "quick wins" prioritized list (1 hour)
- [ ] Export as PDF for client
- [ ] Internal review: is this actionable?

Week 4: Follow-up
- [ ] Send report + calendar link for follow-up call
- [ ] 1-hour follow-up call: present top 5 recommendations, answer questions
- [ ] Send final checklist for their team to follow

Total time: ~9 hours over 4 weeks
```

## Exercise 5: Lock in Your Version 1 Spec

**Time: 30 minutes**

You now have:
- Product design (Exercise 1)
- Scope validation (Exercise 2)
- Pricing (Exercise 3)
- Delivery process (Exercise 4)

Create a one-page spec sheet summarizing all of this. This is your "Product 1.0" definition.

**Template:**

```markdown
# [Product Name]

## What It Solves
[Problem statement]

## Who It's For
[ICP: company type + person]

## What's Included
[5-7 bullet points of deliverables]

## Timeline
[Total duration]

## Price
€ [amount]

## How It Works
[5-step process]

## Success Criteria
[What the customer gets: metrics, output, outcomes]

## Process & Timing
[Week-by-week breakdown]

## Delivery Tools
[What you'll use to deliver this]
```

**This becomes your sales document.** Print it. Share it with your first customer. Update it after each delivery.

## Reflection Questions

1. **Confidence:** On a scale of 1–10, how confident are you that you can deliver this spec consistently?
2. **Profit:** After delivery costs, will you make 50%+ margin? (If not, raise price or reduce scope)
3. **Scalability:** Could you hire someone to deliver this (partially or fully) in 6 months?
4. **Desirability:** After designing this, do you still want to sell it?

---

## Common Mistakes in Phase 3

❌ **Over-scoping** — Including everything to "wow" the customer. That's why they never finish.

❌ **Under-pricing** — Charging so little that you resent the work or can't afford to do it well.

❌ **Not documenting the process** — "I'll remember how I did it." You won't.

❌ **Forgetting the founder price** — Charging full price to your first customer. Get testimonials first.

❌ **Not stress-testing scope** — Not realistically assessing if you can do this 3 times in 60 days.

## How to Know You're Ready for Phase 4

✅ You have a detailed product/service spec
✅ You've stress-tested the scope (realistically can do it 3x in 60 days)

✅ You have a detailed week-by-week delivery process
✅ You know what tools/templates you need

If you have all 5 checkmarks, you're ready for [Phase 4: Delivery](./04-delivery.md).
