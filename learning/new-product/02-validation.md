# Phase 2: Validation — Testing Demand with Real Prospects

**Duration:** 7–14 days
**Goal:** Confirm that 30+ prospects want this solution and would pay for it
**Output:** Validated ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) + proof of market demand

---

## Why This Phase Matters

Validation is the difference between building something people want and wasting 2 months on a product nobody needs. This phase is simple: **talk to 30 prospects and ask them directly if they have the problem and would pay.**

Don't build anything yet. Just talk.

---

## The Validation Workflow

1. **Define your target audience** (based on Phase 1)
2. **Find 30 prospects** on LinkedIn or in communities
3. **Send a personalized message** (not a pitch)
4. **Qualify who has the problem** (simple criteria)
5. **Ask the money question** ("Would you pay for this?")
6. **Score your results** (how many confirmed the problem?)

**Milestone:** If 15+ out of 30 say "yes, I have this problem," you have validation. Move to Phase 3.

**Pivot:** If fewer than 10 say yes, the problem isn't validated. Pick a different problem or adjust your ICP.

## Exercise 1: Define Your Target Audience (ICP)

**Time: 30 minutes**

Create a detailed profile of your ideal first customer:

**Company Profile:**
- Industry/vertical
- Company size (team members, revenue)
- Stage (startup, growth, mature)
- Tech stack or tools they use

**Decision Maker Profile:**
- Job title (VP Engineering, Tech Lead, Founder, etc.)
- Responsibilities
- Budget authority?
- Pain points they personally deal with

**Problem Severity:**
- How urgent is this problem? (nice-to-have or must-solve?)
- Who else in the company feels this pain?
- How much time do they waste on this weekly?

**Buying Pattern:**
- Budget range for a solution like this?
- Decision process (quick or slow?)
- Who else needs to approve?

**Template:**

```
**Ideal First Customer Profile:**

Company:
- Industry: ___________________
- Size: ___________________
- Stage: ___________________
- Tech: ___________________

Person:
- Title: ___________________
- Responsibilities: ___________________
- Pain point: ___________________

Problem Severity:
- How often: [Daily/Weekly/Monthly]
- Time waste per week: ___ hours
- Cost impact: [High/Medium/Low]
- Urgency: [Critical/High/Medium]

Buying:
- Budget: €___ to €___
- Decision speed: [1-2 weeks / 1 month / 3+ months]
- Approvers: ___________________
```

**Real Example:**

```
Company:
- Industry: Web development agencies (5-20 people)
- Size: 8-15 people
- Stage: Profitable, stable
- Tech: Laravel, MySQL, basic AWS

Person:
- Title: CTO or Tech Lead
- Responsibilities: Managing projects, code quality, deployment
- Pain point: Broken deployments, performance issues, technical debt

Problem Severity:
- How often: Daily (1-2 issues per week)
- Time waste: 5-10 hours/week debugging
- Cost: €2k-5k per incident (downtime, client complaints)
- Urgency: Critical (losing customers)

Buying:
- Budget: €1,000-2,500 for a quick fix
- Decision speed: 1-2 weeks (CTO decides)
- Approvers: CTO + Owner (for budget > €2,000)
```

## Exercise 2: Find 30 Prospects

**Time: 3–5 hours**

Use LinkedIn to find 30 people matching your ICP profile.

**LinkedIn Search Strategy:**

1. Search by job title: "VP Engineering" OR "CTO" OR "Tech Lead" (your target roles)
2. Filter by industry (if applicable): "Web Development" OR "SaaS"
3. Filter by company size: "11-50" employees (example)
4. Filter by location (if relevant)
5. Sort by "Most recent activity" (more active users)

**Create a tracking spreadsheet:**

|------|---------|-------|--------------|--------|-------|
| John Smith | Acme Inc | CTO | linkedin.com/... | Messaged | Has Laravel |


**Don't overthink this.** Your first list doesn't need to be perfect. You'll find better prospects as you go.

## Exercise 3: Craft Your Outreach Message

**Time: 30 minutes**

Write a short, personalized message. The goal is NOT to sell. It's to start a conversation about their problem.

**The formula:**

```
Hi [Name],

[Genuine compliment or connection point] 

[Question about their problem]

[Why you're asking]

—

[Your name]
```

**Key rules:**
- ✅ Personalized (mention something specific about them or their company)
- ✅ Short (3–4 sentences max)
- ✅ Ask a question (don't pitch)
- ✅ Solve for the person, not the company
- ❌ No link to your website
- ❌ No "let me show you a solution"
- ❌ No asking for a call immediately

**Real Examples:**

**Example 1 (API Performance Product):**
```
Hi Maria,

I saw you recently posted about Laravel performance. I've spent the last 18 months optimizing APIs for agencies dealing with the exact same database and caching issues.

Quick question: when you hit performance issues on a project, how long does it typically take your team to diagnose and fix them?

Curious because I'm talking to agencies about this specifically.

— Alex
```

**Example 2 (Legacy Code Audit):**
```
Hi James,

Your CTO bio mentions your team handles legacy migrations—that's a tough area. I've helped 3 agencies untangle old monolithic code and plan safe rewrites.

One question: when you inherit a messy codebase, what's usually the biggest unknown? (Technical debt? Security issues? Time to refactor?)

— Sarah
```

**Example 3 (Deployment Automation):**
```
Hi Chris,

I noticed Acme has 15 developers. With that team size, deployment coordination must be challenging.

Do you currently handle deployments manually, or have you automated them? Either way, what's your biggest friction point?

— David
```

**Exercise: Write 3 versions of your message.**

Personalize each for a different company/person type, but keep the core structure.

## Exercise 4: Send Messages & Track Responses

**Time: 7–10 days**

Send your message to all 30 prospects over 7–10 days (3–4 messages per day, not all at once).

**For each response:**

- **No reply in 3–5 days:** They're not interested. Mark as "No reply."
- **"Not my problem":** Mark as "Not relevant."
- **"Sounds interesting, but...":** This is good. They're curious. Ask your problem confirmation question.
- **"Yes, I have this problem":** Excellent. Move to Exercise 5.

**Template to track:**

```
Person: ___________________
Date Messaged: ___________________
Response Type: [Replied / No reply / Not relevant / Interested]
Quote: "___________________"
Next Step: [Follow-up / Discovery call / Not interested]
```

**Follow-up rule:** If they don't reply in 3–5 days, send ONE follow-up. If still no reply, move on.

## Exercise 5: Qualify and Ask the Money Question

**Time: 2–3 hours**

When someone responds positively, you have a short window. Send ONE more message asking:

1. **Confirm the problem** ("So when you say X, do you mean...?")
2. **Ask about current solution** ("How are you solving this now?")
3. **Ask the money question** ("If someone could help with this, what would that be worth to you?")

**The Money Question Script:**

```
Got it. So the challenge is [their problem summary].

Quick follow-up: if someone could help you solve this reliably, what would that be worth in your budget? (No pressure—just trying to understand the value.)
```

**What to listen for:**

- ✅ "We'd pay €1,000–2,000"  → They have budget
- ✅ "€500–1,500" → Real interest, smaller budget
- ❌ "Free would be nice" → Not qualified
- ❌ "Maybe later" → Not now
- ✅ "I'd need approval, but definitely" → Real problem

**Record this:**

```
Person: ___________________
Confirmed Problem: [Yes/No] - Quote: "___________________"
Current Solution: ___________________
Budget: €___ to €___
Likelihood to Buy: [High / Medium / Low]
Notes: ___________________
```

## Exercise 6: Tally Your Results

**Time: 30 minutes**

After messaging 30 prospects, count:

```
Total contacted: 30
Replied: ___
Confirmed the problem: ___
Willing to pay: ___
Budget range indicated: €___ to €___

Conversion rates:
- Reply rate: ___% (replied / contacted)
- Problem confirmation: ___% (confirmed / replied)
- Purchase intent: ___% (willing to pay / confirmed problem)
```

**Validation thresholds:**

| Result | Interpretation | Next Step |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| 15+ confirmed problem | Strong demand ✅ | → Phase 3 (Design) |
| 10-14 confirmed | Decent signal | → Refine ICP or problem, message 15 more |
| <10 confirmed | Weak demand ❌ | → Go back to Phase 1, pick new problem |

## Reflection Questions

1. **Confidence in demand:** On a scale of 1–10, how confident are you that your ICP actually has this problem?
2. **Market size:** Do you think there are 100+ companies matching this profile? 500+?
3. **Competitor check:** Did any of them mention existing solutions they're already using?
4. **Pricing reality:** Were the budgets you heard realistic? Higher/lower than you expected?

---

## Common Mistakes in Phase 2

❌ **Messaging all 30 people at once** — Space it out. You'll get better at it.

❌ **"No reply = no interest"** — Try one follow-up, then move on.

❌ **Pitching instead of asking** — You're researching, not selling yet.

❌ **Accepting "maybe" or "sounds interesting" as validation** — You need "yes, I have this problem."

❌ **Not tracking responses** — You'll forget who said what. Use the template.

---

## How to Know You're Ready for Phase 3

✅ You contacted 30+ prospects
✅ At least 15 confirmed they have the problem
✅ At least 5 said they'd pay for a solution
✅ You have a clear refined ICP (company type + person)
✅ You know the budget range (€X–Y)

If you have all 5 checkmarks, you're ready for [Phase 3: Design](./03-design.md).

If you have fewer than 15 confirmations, go back to Phase 1 and pick a different problem.
