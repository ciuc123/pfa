# Content Pillars (initial hypotheses)

This file documents the five initial content pillars. Each pillar is a hypothesis to be tested during the validation phase.

1) AI Coding Experiments

- Purpose: Test how modern AI tools behave on real engineering tasks and document unexpected outcomes.
- Possible topics: multi-agent comparisons, AI refactoring, AI debugging, AI-generated tests, AI-assisted architectures.
- Example titles: "I Let 3 AI Agents Build the Same Laravel API", "I Gave Codex a Real Laravel Project".
- Why it might work: novelty + actionable demos with obvious before/after comparisons.
- Risks: tool churn; content becomes dated quickly; can be noisy if shallow.
- How to test: produce 6–8 experiments with clear metrics (time saved, bugs found, quality of output).

2) Real Developer Experiments

- Purpose: Run practical experiments on architectures, infra costs, migrations and show real consequences.
- Possible topics: migrating off AWS, running a site for €0/month, replacing parts of a stack.
- Example titles: "I Tried Hosting a Website for €0/month", "Can Cloudflare Replace My Existing Stack?".
- Why it might work: tangible stakes and relatable constraints for devs.
- Risks: results are context-specific; compression into a single video can oversimplify.
- How to test: pick small experiments that complete within 1–2 weeks and produce measurable outcomes.

3) Laravel / PHP

- Purpose: Provide opinionated, practical stories and experiments related to Laravel and PHP without becoming a tutorial farm.
- Possible topics: architecture stories, migrating, common mistakes, performance experiments.
- Example titles: "10 Laravel Mistakes I Don't Make Anymore", "What Happens When You Give AI a Huge Laravel Codebase?".
- Why it might work: targeted to an enthusiastic, monetizable niche while remaining relevant to wider backend audience.
- Risks: over-narrow audience if content is pure tutorials.
- How to test: blend experiment episodes with opinion pieces and measure engagement per video.

4) Building Products

- Purpose: Chronicle product experiments, MVP builds, and decisions to show the creator's product thinking and execution.
- Possible topics: building a SaaS in 7 days, product validation with free tools, pricing experiments.
- Example titles: "I Tried Building a SaaS in 7 Days", "Can AI Build a Real SaaS?".
- Why it might work: appeals to indie hackers and developers who want to monetize skills.
- Risks: long-running builds may take too long for validation; building without audience evidence wastes time.
- How to test: prefer micro-SaaS / 1–2 week experiments that can be shown end-to-end.

5) Developer Business

- Purpose: Share practical lessons on freelancing, pricing, client work and transitioning to products.
- Possible topics: first €1,000 freelancing, 30-day client outreach, case studies.
- Example titles: "How I'd Make My First €1,000 Freelancing", "I Tried Getting Freelance Clients for 30 Days".
- Why it might work: direct monetization audience; useful for sponsorships and affiliate products.
- Risks: market crowded; must be specific and backed by real outcomes.
- How to test: run small documented experiments, publish clear outcomes and process videos.

General note

These pillars are starting hypotheses. Analytics (views, click-through, retention, comments) should determine which pillars scale. Each pillar must produce repeatable formats (experiments, follow-ups, short lessons).
