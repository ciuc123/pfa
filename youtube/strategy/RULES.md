# Operating Rules

Practical rules to keep the channel focused and maintainable during the validation phase.

1. Don't spend money before validating the concept.
2. Don't judge the strategy from one or two videos.
3. Prefer original experiments and real projects over generic summaries.
4. Use existing development projects as content sources when possible.
5. Don't copy competitors; learn from them and adapt.
6. Don't chase every trend — test a few and double down on what works.
7. Don't spend hours polishing a video that hasn't been validated by audience signaling.
8. Don't rely on AI to pump out content without human verification and narrative.
9. Track hypotheses and evidence separately (use `research/LEARNINGS.md`).
10. Review patterns across multiple videos before making strategic pivots.
11. Optimize for learning before optimizing for revenue in the early phase.
12. Don't abandon a strategy because of a single bad result.
13. Don't become trapped by the initial niche — be ready to generalize if the audience grows.
14. Protect the sustainability of the workflow: keep the cadence realistic.

Things I should probably NOT do

- Generic AI news roundups without original experiments
- Mass-produced AI-voice videos with no personal input
- Stock-footage spam and low-effort listicles
- Copying scripts or formats verbatim from other creators
- Clickbait titles without substance
- Spending heavily on tools before validation
- Building a SaaS for months without audience evidence
- Optimizing purely for subscriber count over useful audience signals

Short checklist before publishing

- Does the video have a clear stake and demonstrable outcome?
- Can I show something that changed because of the experiment?
- Is the production level good enough to test the idea (not perfect)?
- Have I recorded the hypothesis and the expected evidence?
