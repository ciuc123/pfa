# Audience

Primary audience (core)

- English-speaking technical people who build backend systems: backend developers, Laravel/PHP developers, full‑stack devs, indie hackers and technical founders.

Secondary audience

- Developers curious about AI-assisted coding, developer productivity, small product experiments, and transition-to-creator topics.

Problems this audience faces

- Uncertainty about whether new tools (AI agents, code assistants) actually help on real projects.
- Time and resource constraints for prototyping or experimenting with infrastructure changes.
- Need for practical advice they can apply immediately, not marketing claims.
- Desire to learn how to turn developer skills into income or product ideas.

What they're curious about

- Can AI really help me ship features faster or fix bugs?
- How do real projects behave when reworked with AI or new stacks?
- Is Laravel/PHP still viable for building products today?
- How do I build small, profitable developer products or consulting offerings?

What they might pay for (hypotheses)

- Practical templates / starter kits (Laravel starters, infra templates)
- Short, focused guides on AI-assisted development workflows
- Tools or scripts that save repeated setup time
- Consulting or hands-on help (validation, architecture reviews)

Content that might attract and retain them

- Real experiments with clear stakes and measurable outcomes
- Post-mortems showing what failed and what worked
- Short, focused deep dives demonstrating a single idea
- Reusable assets, starter repos or templates (after validation)

Assumptions (labelled as hypotheses)

- Hypothesis: Demonstrations of real projects using AI attract more engaged viewers than pure tutorials.
- Hypothesis: Developer audiences prefer specific experiments with clear outcomes rather than broad lists or marketing summaries.

What makes them return

- Consistent cadence of experiment-style videos
- A mix of novelty (AI experiments) and predictable formats (weekly post-mortems, follow-ups)
- Clear value: either learning that changes how they work or curiosity satisfied by a surprising result.
