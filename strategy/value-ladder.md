# Value Ladder — your pricing path

You don't sell one thing. You sell a path: free → entry → core → ongoing. Each level leads to the next. Start at the bottom (free) and move up as you build proof.

---

## The ladder

| Level | What it is | Price | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free** | Value-first asset: checklist, mini-audit, Loom teardown, existing blog post | €0 | Give value before asking. Build trust. Start conversations. |
| **Entry** | Audit / review / assessment (from `strategy/offers/`) | €750–2,000 | Low-risk first engagement. Prove you can deliver. |
| **Core** | Implementation sprint (fix the top issues from the audit) | €2,500–6,000 | The real work. Bigger commitment, bigger outcome. |
| **Ongoing** | Fractional senior backend / advisory / maintenance retainer | €1,500–3,500/mo | Recurring revenue. You become their go-to person. |

---

## How to use it

1. **Free first.** Every prospect gets a free value-first asset before you ask for anything. This is the "jab" (Gary V) and the lead magnet (Hormozi). See `strategy/free-value-assets.md`.

2. **Entry offer next.** The free asset leads to the audit. "I found 3 risks in your codebase — want the full audit?" This is your first paid engagement. See `strategy/offers/`.

3. **Core after that.** Once the audit is delivered, offer to implement the top fixes. "I found the problems. Want me to fix the top 3?" You already have trust from the audit — this is the natural upsell.

4. **Ongoing last.** After implementation, offer a monthly retainer. "I'll be your on-demand senior backend person for X hours/month." This is where real revenue lives.

---

## Pricing rules (Hormozi)

- **First 3 clients:** charge founder/pilot price (€500–750 for the first audit). Goal is proof, not profit. Get a testimonial.
- **After 3 case studies:** raise to full entry price (€750–2,000).
- **After 10 clients:** raise to premium pricing (upper range).
- **Price by outcome, not by hour.** "I'll find your top 10 codebase risks in 5 days" is worth more than "I'll spend 5 hours looking at your code."
- **Offer a paid-up-front discount.** "€1,500 for the audit, or €1,200 if you pay upfront." Fast decisions = better clients.
- **Never position yourself as cheap help. Position yourself as senior risk reduction.** You have 15 years of PHP, 10+ years of Laravel, and 6 years of freelance work. That's your differentiator.

## The value equation (Hormozi)

When you package your offer, make it stronger using this formula:

**Value = Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Success ÷ Time Delay ÷ Effort & Sacrifice**

For each offer, ask:
1. **Dream outcome:** What do they actually want? (Not "an audit" — "confidence that their codebase won't break on the next deploy")
2. **Likelihood:** How can you make them believe it will work? (Proof, guarantees, before/after examples)
3. **Time delay:** How fast can you deliver? (5 days, not "whenever")
4. **Effort:** How little do they have to do? (Just give me repo access — I handle the rest)
5. **Bonuses:** What can you add for free that increases perceived value? (Security scan, team onboarding checklist, CI checklist)
6. **Risk reversal:** What's the risk for them? (If I don't find 3 actionable issues, you don't pay)

Use this to rewrite your offer from "Laravel Audit" to: "In 5 business days, I'll find the top 10 risks in your codebase and give you a prioritized 30-day fix plan your team can execute. If I don't find 3 critical issues, you don't pay."
